# Equipment Monitoring Guidelines

Fictional training document. All alert codes, indices, and bands are invented for a university simulation and do not represent real equipment limits.

## Severity bands

Station 41 reports condition using the invented Condition Index (CI), a unitless score. Readings fall into three bands. Band Green means normal monitoring. Band Amber means abnormal: verification and escalation are required. Band Red means critical: the Emergency Response Procedure applies.

## Alert W-217: drive-end vibration drift

Alert W-217 means the drive-end Vibration Condition Index on a compressor unit has moved away from its baseline. W-217 is always an abnormal condition, even if the unit sounds normal and the reading later falls. W-217 must never be treated as a sensor nuisance alert without verification by the Station Reliability Engineer.

## Alert W-305: seal gas differential

Alert W-305 means the seal gas differential display has left its expected band. W-305 is an abnormal condition and requires escalation under the Anomaly Escalation Procedure.

## Alert W-412: lube oil condition

Alert W-412 means the lube oil condition score has declined. W-412 requires a recorded check and escalation to the Shift Supervisor before the end of the current shift.

## Alert G-118: area gas detector pre-alarm

Alert G-118 means an area gas detector reports a pre-alarm. G-118 is always treated as Band Red and the Emergency Response Procedure applies immediately.

## Recording readings

Every abnormal reading is recorded in the Station Log with the time, unit, alert code, and reading. Alerts must not be cleared or acknowledged away to stop them from displaying.
